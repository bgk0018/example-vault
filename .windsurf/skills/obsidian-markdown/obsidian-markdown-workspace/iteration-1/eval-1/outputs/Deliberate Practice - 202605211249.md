# Deliberate Practice

Deliberate practice is a structured, purposeful form of practice distinct from naive repetition.[^1] Where casual practice involves mindless repetition — playing the same song the same way — deliberate practice requires focused attention on specific weaknesses, immediate feedback, and tasks designed just beyond current ability.

The concept challenges the popular notion that talent is innate. [[Talent is Overrated - 202304261300|Talent is Overrated]] — what looks like natural gift is almost always the result of thousands of hours of deliberate, structured effort. Ericsson's research across domains (music, chess, sports) consistently shows that ==experts aren't born; they're built through deliberate practice==.

Key components:
- **Well-defined goals** — not "get better at piano" but "play measures 12-16 at tempo without errors"
- **Full attention** — deliberate practice is mentally demanding and cannot be done on autopilot
- **Immediate feedback** — from a teacher, recording, or self-assessment
- **Operating at the edge** — tasks should stretch just beyond current competence

This connects to the idea of [[Flow - 202304261255|Flow]] — deliberate practice often occurs at the boundary where challenge meets skill, though it is typically less enjoyable than flow states because of its emphasis on confronting weaknesses.
