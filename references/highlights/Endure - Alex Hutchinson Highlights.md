## Highlights

### Part I: The Limits of Endurance
>[!QUOTE]  
>The brain decides in advance how hard you can push, based on its calculations of what's safe — and it always leaves a reserve. (Location 342) #✂️  ^ref-100001

---
>[!QUOTE]  
>Progressive overload — the principle that you must gradually increase the demands on your body to force adaptation — is the foundation of all effective training. But the adaptations aren't purely muscular. Your brain learns to tolerate greater levels of discomfort. (Location 518) #✂️  ^ref-100002

---
>[!QUOTE]  
>Fatigue is not simply the failure of muscles. It is a complex, regulated process in which the brain plays a central role, constantly monitoring signals from the body and the environment. (Location 687) #✂️  ^ref-100003

---
### Part II: The Central Governor
>[!QUOTE]  
>Tim Noakes proposed the central governor theory: the idea that the brain acts as a governor, limiting physical output not because the muscles have failed, but to protect the body from catastrophic damage. (Location 1024) #✂️  ^ref-100004

---
>[!QUOTE]  
>The governor doesn't just respond to physiological signals — it also incorporates psychological factors like motivation, competition, and even knowledge of how far you have left to go. (Location 1156) #✂️  ^ref-100005

---
### Part III: Training the Mind
>[!QUOTE]  
>If the brain is the ultimate limiter, then training the brain — through deliberate exposure to discomfort, mental rehearsal, and motivational strategies — is as important as training the body. (Location 1589) #✂️  ^ref-100006
