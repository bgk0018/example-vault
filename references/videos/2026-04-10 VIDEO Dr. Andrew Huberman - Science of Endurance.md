# Science of Endurance

>[!summary]- Description
> Dr. Andrew Huberman discusses the neuroscience behind endurance performance — how the brain regulates effort, the role of dopamine in motivation during sustained effort, and practical protocols for improving endurance through both physical and mental training.

## Key Takeaways

- The brain's assessment of effort is not purely physiological — dopamine levels modulate perceived exertion
- This supports the [[Central Governor Theory - 202604201415|central governor theory]]: the brain actively regulates output
- [[Progressive Overload - 202604151030|Progressive overload]] has a neurological component — repeated exposure to discomfort trains the brain to tolerate more
- Zone 2 training builds mitochondrial density, but also trains the brain to sustain low-level effort for extended periods
- [[Sleep and Recovery - 202605011200|Sleep]] deprivation increases perceived exertion at the same absolute workload — the governor becomes more conservative
