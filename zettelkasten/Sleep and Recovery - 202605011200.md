# Sleep and Recovery

Sleep is the single most effective recovery tool available to athletes. During deep (NREM) sleep, the body releases growth hormone, repairs muscle tissue, and consolidates motor learning. REM sleep processes emotional stress from training and competition.

Sleep deprivation has measurable performance effects:
- Reaction time degrades after just one night of poor sleep
- Muscle protein synthesis drops by up to 18% with chronic sleep restriction
- Perceived exertion increases — the same workout feels harder on less sleep
- Injury risk increases significantly when athletes average less than 7 hours

The relationship between sleep and the [[Central Governor Theory - 202604201415|Central Governor Theory]] is interesting: a sleep-deprived brain may set more conservative limits on output, essentially "turning down the governor" as a protective mechanism.
