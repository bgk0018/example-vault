# Does Cold Exposure Improve Recovery?

## Content

Cold water immersion (ice baths, cold plunges) is widely used by athletes for post-exercise recovery. But the evidence is surprisingly mixed.

**Arguments for:**
- Reduces perceived muscle soreness (DOMS) in the 24-48 hours after intense exercise
- May reduce inflammation markers in the short term
- Psychological benefit — athletes *feel* recovered, which may influence the [[Central Governor Theory - 202604201415|central governor]]

**Arguments against:**
- Cold exposure blunts the inflammatory response that *drives* adaptation — you may recover faster but adapt less
- Studies show cold water immersion after strength training reduces hypertrophy gains over time
- The placebo effect is hard to control for (you know when you're sitting in ice water)

**Open questions:**
- Is there a timing window where cold exposure helps recovery without blunting adaptation?
- Does the answer differ for endurance vs. strength athletes?
- How does cold exposure interact with [[Sleep and Recovery - 202605011200|sleep quality]]?
