# Progressive Overload

Progressive overload is the foundational principle of all effective training: you must gradually increase the demands placed on the body to force adaptation.[^1] But the adaptations aren't purely muscular — the brain also learns to tolerate greater levels of discomfort, which connects to the [[Central Governor Theory - 202604201415|Central Governor Theory]].[^2]

This means that progressive overload works on two fronts simultaneously:
1. **Physiological** — muscle fibers grow, capillary density increases, mitochondria multiply
2. **Neurological** — the brain's "safety governor" recalibrates its limits based on repeated exposure to higher loads

The implication is that training programs that only focus on physical load progression are missing half the picture. Mental rehearsal, competition, and deliberate discomfort exposure all contribute to overload at the neurological level.
